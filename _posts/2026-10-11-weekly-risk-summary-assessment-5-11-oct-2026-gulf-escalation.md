## Top threat of the week: escalation in the Gulf

On Monday 5 October, Kuwait's General Staff said its air defences were engaging hostile drone attacks from Iran, after Iran's Revolutionary Guards claimed to have struck targets in Kuwait; explosions heard were attributed to interception activity [[1]](https://globalthreatsignal.org/threat/kuwait-reports-iranian-drone-attacks-as-air-defenses-engage-59078). Two days later, on 7 October, the UK Maritime Trade Operations agency reported that a tanker was hit by multiple projectiles about 94 kilometres north of Madinat ash Shamal, inside Qatar's exclusive economic zone. UKMTO reported an unspecified number of casualties and did not identify the vessel's origin or those responsible; the source situates the attack amid heightened regional conflict and a rise in attacks on vessels in and around the Strait of Hormuz [[2]](https://globalthreatsignal.org/threat/tanker-struck-by-multiple-projectiles-off-qatar-s-northern-coast-59629).

**Assessment.** A claimed state-on-state drone strike against a Gulf Cooperation Council member and a casualty-causing attack on commercial shipping represent the week's most consequential development. The likelihood of further incidents affecting Gulf states and maritime traffic over the coming week is judged **high**, and the potential impact **severe**, given the region's weight in energy and shipping flows. The economic transmission channel is already visible elsewhere in the feed: the IMF cited higher energy prices and supply disruptions among the risks to Pakistan's recovery [[8]](https://globalthreatsignal.org/threat/pakistan-reaches-imf-staff-level-agreement-for-1-21-billion-in-financing-59626), and Canada's banking regulator OSFI warned that geopolitical uncertainty has intensified risks to the financial system [[9]](https://globalthreatsignal.org/threat/osfi-warns-geopolitical-economic-and-ai-risks-to-canada-s-financial-system-have-59673).
